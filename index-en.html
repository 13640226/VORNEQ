01 / REVOLT OF REASON

Revolt of Reason

MANIFESTO

This is not a shelter built to soothe tired minds. It was not made to place a comforting bandage over the wounds of easy certainty.

Saman Kherad was not built for obedience. We will not place pre-chewed truth in your mouth, nor replace the chain of an old belief with the chain of a new one.

The aim is to awaken the steady will of reason:

  • Separate evidence from the noise of those who make claims.
  • See the roots of power and interest behind every assumption.
  • Do not fear the darkness of not knowing; say plainly: I decide for myself.

Adversarial questions

Against every claim, raise these questions:

  • Where did this claim come from?
  • What living evidence supports it?
  • Who benefits if it is accepted blindly?
  • Who pays the cost of accepting it?
  • What uncomfortable truth may be hidden by this framing?
“I don't know” is not weakness. It is refuge for a mind that refuses the humiliation of false certainty.

Your belief is not your identity. You may have accepted an idea yesterday and place it under review today without destroying yourself.

No book, leader, government, religion, media outlet, majority, or machine is immune from your questioning.

Even Saman Kherad itself must be able to burn in the fire of your criticism.

02 / PRINCIPLES OF POWER

Foundational Principles of Reason

These are not commandments. They are tools for cutting through illusion.

0

PRINCIPLE ZERO

No power is sacred

Power does not sit only in government. It hides in wealth, fame, tradition, family structures, and even in blind trust toward people close to you.

Who benefits? Who pays? Who faces the greatest cost for saying “no”?

1

PRINCIPLE ONE

Nothing is immune from doubt

Sacredness becomes dangerous when it turns questioning into a crime. Wherever asking is called betrayal or sin, notice the fence.

Truth does not fear questions. If a claim collapses under one, let it collapse.

2

PRINCIPLE TWO

Evidence sets the level of confidence

A loud voice, respectable face, rank, clothing, fame, or faith is not evidence by itself.

Certainty does not change reality. Where is the source? Can the claim be tested independently?

3

PRINCIPLE THREE

Tear up the labels

Do not imprison people inside nationality, religion, party, or their past.

Fight ideas without mercy; treat people fairly. Another person's identity is not your instrument of control.

4

PRINCIPLE FOUR

Reject digital chains

No structure that claims to support freedom should bind you through fear, guilt, or addiction.

No points. No daily streaks. If an idea matters, it should survive without holding your attention hostage.

03 / RAZOR OF DOUBT

A Six-Step Method for Testing Claims

No ready-made answers. A method of examination.

  1. 01

    Expose the claim

    What exactly is being asserted? Separate fact, interpretation, and value judgment.

  2. 02

    Interrogate the evidence

    Where does it come from? What is the quality of the data? Where is the contrary evidence?

  3. 03

    Mark the boundary of ignorance

    Do not pretend to know. Where does your knowledge end and uncertainty begin?

  4. 04

    Trace the power

    Where do money, incentives, fear, and structures of power stand in this story?

  5. 05

    Count the cost of the choice

    If you accept or reject the claim, what do you gain, lose, or risk?

  6. 06

    Make the final judgment yourself

    Do not outsource responsibility for your thinking—even to experts. The responsibility belongs to your mind.

04 / QUESTION HALL

The Hall of Identity Testing

Even courageous people can fear confronting their own biases. Choose a belief you cling to—the one whose loss would leave you feeling empty.

If no evidence could ever break that belief, then you no longer own the idea; the idea owns you.

If I saw [ ................................... ] with my own eyes, I would place the belief I cling to under review.

This sentence is not stored on any server. This is a confrontation between you and the mirror.

Expose hidden power and do not hide earlier versions of error.
