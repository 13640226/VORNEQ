# VORNEQ Brand Constitution

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved  
**Date:** 2026-09-07

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the stable, normative brand principles for VORNEQ. It governs how the brand should be understood, expressed, and protected across product, engineering, communications, documentation, marketing, partnerships, and institutional use.

The Brand Constitution is intentionally durable. Execution details may evolve, but changes to the principles in this document require deliberate review and approval.

## 2. Core Brand Thesis

> **VORNEQ does not manufacture trust. It makes the grounds for trust inspectable.**

VORNEQ is trust infrastructure for the digital economy. Its purpose is to reduce the cost of establishing trust in digital exchanges without becoming the authority that dictates truth.

## 3. Trust Philosophy

VORNEQ is governed by four foundational principles:

1. **Trust is not a score.** It is context supported by inspectable evidence.
2. **Verification is not truth.** It is an attributable process and result.
3. **Consequential claims should be inspectable, contestable, and attributable.**
4. **VORNEQ lowers the cost of establishing trust without becoming the authority that dictates it.**

These principles apply to product design, data modeling, reputation systems, verification workflows, evidence handling, AI-assisted features, APIs, and public communication.

## 4. Interaction Model

VORNEQ uses the following interaction model:

**Discover → Verify → Transact → Deliver → Attest**

This is an interaction model, not a mandatory state machine. Products and workflows may enter, exit, or omit stages where appropriate.

## 5. Core Concepts

The VORNEQ ecosystem may express trust through the following concepts:

- **Artifact** — a digital object, record, work, item, or claim-bearing entity.
- **Identity** — an attributable person, organization, account, or recognized entity.
- **Verification** — an attributable verification process and result.
- **Evidence** — inspectable material supporting or challenging a claim.
- **Entitlement** — a right, permission, access condition, ownership relation, or licensed capability.
- **Reputation** — contextual evidence about prior behavior, never a universal human value or global trust score.

Free-text authorship, names, or descriptions must not automatically be inferred as verified Identity relationships.

## 7. Brand Character

VORNEQ should consistently feel:

- Precise
- Calm
- Intelligent
- Architectural
- Trustworthy
- Institutional
- Contemporary
- Global
- Premium
- Technically rigorous
- Human-centered
- Timeless

The brand should communicate:

**Trust without bureaucracy. Intelligence without complexity. Technology without coldness. Authority without arrogance.**

VORNEQ should never feel noisy, speculative, gimmicky, childish, aggressively futuristic, or visually dependent on technology clichés.

## 8. Voice and Language

VORNEQ communicates with a voice that is:

- illuminating rather than promotional;
- direct rather than theatrical;
- respectful rather than paternalistic;
- professional, clear, and fact-based;
- confident without claiming absolute authority.

Claims about verification, trust, evidence, reputation, or authenticity must remain precise. VORNEQ must not imply that verification equals truth, that reputation is universally transferable across contexts, or that evidence eliminates legitimate disagreement.

## 9. Right to an Inspectable Record

VORNEQ supports a **Right to an Inspectable Record**: consequential digital interactions should be capable of producing attributable, inspectable evidence where appropriate.

Inspectable records may be tamper-evident, signed, or independently attestable, but they are not presumed immutable by default. Retention, visibility, consent, correction, deletion, confidentiality, and applicable legal obligations remain explicit design concerns.

## 10. Trust as Infrastructure

VORNEQ treats trust as infrastructure rather than a manufactured commodity.

Trust can be costly to establish. Evidence, attribution, verification, and inspectable records can reduce that cost. VORNEQ must not introduce a universal “Trust Asset,” “Trust Unit,” or equivalent global score that converts trust into a single fungible quantity.

Independent attestations may come from institutions, experts, authorities, users, systems, or AI-assisted processes, provided provenance, domain, time, evidence, and contestability remain visible.

## 11. Symbol Architecture

The VORNEQ identity system distinguishes between the independent **Symbol** and broader logo/signature configurations.

### Final Symbol Candidate — Status

- **Current Status:** Final Symbol Candidate
- **Geometric Reference:** B1-b3 (Distinctive)
- **Geometry Status:** Frozen; no redesign or structural change without a new technical or legal reason.
- **Similarity Review:** Pending
- **Trademark Clearance:** Pending; no clearance claim may be published until actual review has been completed.
- **Next Gate:** Similarity / Trademark Review
- **Master Symbol Status:** Not yet locked

After applicable similarity and trademark review is completed successfully, the status may be changed to **Master Symbol — Locked**, with the lock date recorded and the wording **“Clearance confirmed where applicable”** used only where factually supported.

The term **First Used** is not used as a substitute for legal clearance or for the Master Symbol lock date.

## 12. Master Symbol Governance

Once the Final Symbol Candidate becomes the Master Symbol, the canonical symbol must not be casually redesigned, reinterpreted, replaced, decorated, simplified, distorted, stylized, regenerated, or “improved.”

The following must be preserved:

- silhouette;
- geometry;
- proportions;
- negative space;
- stroke and mass relationships;
- optical balance;
- orientation;
- internal construction;
- relationship between symbol and approved wordmark/signatures.

Permitted changes after lock are limited to controlled technical adaptations that preserve the canonical geometry, such as approved positive/reversed assets, export formats, pixel-hinting, or narrowly documented optical corrections required for extreme small-size rendering.

## 13. Logo Architecture

The eventual VORNEQ logo system may include:

1. **Primary Signature** — Symbol + VORNEQ Wordmark
2. **Institutional Signature** — Symbol + VORNEQ Wordmark + “Trust Infrastructure”
3. **Compact Signature** — approved constrained-layout configuration
4. **Micro Mark** — Master Symbol optimized for small approved digital contexts without changing canonical identity
5. **Wordmark** — approved VORNEQ typographic mark without the Symbol where context requires it

The Master Symbol remains the principal independent recognition asset.

- **Cloud:** `#F8FAFC`

### Semantic Colors

- **Success:** `#15803D`
- **Warning:** `#D97706`

These values establish the constitutional palette direction. Detailed color roles, accessibility pairings, production tokens, print conversions, and final VORNEQ Blue calibration belong in the visual identity specification and may be refined without weakening the principles in this Constitution.

The identity must work convincingly in pure black and white before relying on color.

## 15. Typography

VORNEQ typography should be engineered, international, neutral, highly legible, and contemporary.

Preferred direction:

- Inter where appropriate for digital product use;
- premium neo-grotesk or modern geometric sans-serif for approved identity work;
- disciplined hierarchy and strong multilingual legibility.

Avoid:

- decorative display type as a primary system;
- novelty “futuristic” fonts;
- excessively rounded startup typography;
- extreme condensed styles;
- stereotypical techno lettering.

The final VORNEQ Wordmark is a designed identity asset and must not be treated as ordinary typed text once approved.

## 16. Layout and Visual Structure

VORNEQ visual composition should use a disciplined, Swiss-influenced grid with:

- strong alignment;
- intentional whitespace;
- clear hierarchy;
- consistent spacing;
- purposeful asymmetry where useful;
- restraint rather than decorative density.

A 12-column grid is a preferred foundation for major responsive brand and product compositions, while implementation may adapt responsibly to context.

Visual systems may inherit geometry, apertures, directional relationships, or spatial logic from the Master Symbol after the symbol has been locked.

## 17. Visual Identity Constraints

VORNEQ should avoid literal or generic trust/technology clichés, including:

- books used as a generic knowledge logo;
- shields;
- padlocks;
- checkmarks;
- globes;
- handshakes;
- generic blockchain cubes;
- generic network-node diagrams;
- AI sparkles;
- conventional cybersecurity imagery.

The identity should prioritize, in order:

**Ownability → Silhouette → Scalability → Optical Balance → Meaning**

“Ownability” is a design objective, not a legal conclusion. Trademark and similarity clearance remain separate requirements.

## 18. Motion Principles

Brand motion, when used, should communicate continuity, inspection, progression, and trust-building rather than entertainment.

Motion may draw inspiration from:

**Discover → Verify → Transact → Deliver → Attest**

Transitions should be calm, purposeful, reversible where appropriate, and consistent with product accessibility expectations.

## 19. Governance

The VORNEQ Core Team acts as brand guardian.

Material changes to this Brand Constitution require:

1. a documented rationale;
2. an ADR or equivalent governance record where the change affects foundational brand principles;
3. Core Team approval.

Changes to the Master Symbol after lock require exceptional justification and must not occur as routine aesthetic iteration.

## 20. Scope

This Constitution applies to all VORNEQ touchpoints, including:

- web applications;
- mobile applications;
- favicons and application icons;
- dashboards;
- API documentation and developer surfaces;
- verification and evidence interfaces;
- marketplace and knowledge experiences;
- reports and certificates;
- presentations;
- cards and credentials;
- email signatures;
- events and conference materials;
- signage;
- institutional communications;
- future physical or embedded touchpoints.

## 21. Relationship to Other Brand Documents

This Constitution defines the stable rules.

A separate **Logo & Visual Identity Design Brief / Specification** may document execution details such as exact vector assets, clear space, minimum sizes, wordmark construction, color tokens, accessibility combinations, grid applications, motion examples, and production exports.

Execution documents may evolve. They must remain consistent with this Constitution.
